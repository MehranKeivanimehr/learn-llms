## One trick, repeated

A large language model (LLM) — the technology behind AI chatbots — does one thing: it looks at the text so far (the **context**) and guesses what comes next. Then it adds that guess to the context and guesses again. A long answer is just this step, repeated.

## Guesses are probabilities

The guess isn't a single word. It's a **probability for every word the model knows**. After *"The capital of France is"*, "Paris" might get 90% and thousands of other words share the rest.

## Our tiny model

The canvas shows a tiny model that knows 17 words. Each word *pulls* toward the words that usually follow it — "France" pulls hard toward "Paris" — and recent words pull harder. Real LLMs learn billions of such numbers from text, but the output is the same kind of thing.
