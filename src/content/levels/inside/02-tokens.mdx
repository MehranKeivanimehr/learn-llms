## Not letters, not words: tokens

Real LLMs predict **tokens** — chunks of text. A common word like "the" is one token; a rare word is split into pieces like "token" + "izer". Whole words would be too many to list, and single letters would make every sentence very long.

## How a tokenizer learns

Most LLMs use **byte-pair encoding (BPE)**. Start from single characters and repeat: find the pair of neighbours that appears most often, and merge it into one new token everywhere. After thousands of merges, common words become single tokens.

## Why counting letters is hard

Because the model sees tokens, not letters, "how many r's are in *strawberry*?" is surprisingly tricky: the model might see `str` · `aw` · `berry`, with the letters hidden inside.
