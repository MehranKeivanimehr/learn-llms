## Attention is order-blind

Attention compares every word with every other word — but nothing in that comparison says *where* a word is. Shuffle the words and each one gets exactly the same scores. To plain attention, "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" are the same bag of words.

That's a problem: in English, word order tells you who did what to whom.

## Adding positions

The fix is to add a **position signal** to each word's vector before attention runs. The original transformer used **sinusoidal positional encodings**: each position gets a unique pattern of sine and cosine waves at different speeds. Many newer models use other schemes (like *rotary* encodings), but the idea is the same — give attention a way to see *where* each word is.

## Many heads

A single attention pass can only look for one kind of thing. Real transformers run several attention **heads** side by side — often dozens. One head might track who did an action, another might link pronouns to nouns, another might watch punctuation. Their outputs are combined, so each word gets information from all of them.
