## Words need context

Read this: *"The animal didn't cross the street because **it** was tired."* You know "it" means the animal, not the street — streets don't get tired. To understand "it", you had to look at other words.

**Attention** is how a transformer does this. Each word's vector gets updated using information from the other words, and the model *learns* which words are worth looking at.

## Queries, keys and values

Every word produces three vectors:

- a **query** — "what am I looking for?"
- a **key** — "what do I contain?"
- a **value** — "what will I share if someone looks at me?"

For the word "it", the model compares its query with every word's key using a dot product. A big dot product means "this key matches what I'm looking for". The scores are divided by √d (the vector length, to keep numbers stable) and passed through softmax, giving **attention weights** that add up to 1. Finally, "it" collects a weighted mix of everyone's values.

## Many words at once

In a real model, *every* word does this at the same time, each with its own query. And the query, key and value vectors aren't hand-made: they're computed from each word's embedding using matrices the model learned during training.
