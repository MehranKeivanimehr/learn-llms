## Putting the pieces together

You've met the main ideas: tokens become vectors, attention mixes information between words, and position signals keep word order. A **transformer block** packages them into one repeatable unit:

1. **Self-attention** — every word gathers information from the others.
2. **Add & Norm** — add the result back onto the input, then normalize.
3. **MLP** (a small feed-forward neural network) — each word is processed on its own; this is where a lot of the model's "knowledge" is stored.
4. **Add & Norm** — again.

An LLM stacks this block many times — GPT-3 used 96 of them. The output of one block is the input of the next.

## Why "Add"?

The **residual connection** adds each sub-layer's output to its input instead of replacing it. Each block only has to learn a *change* to the vectors, and information from early layers can flow straight through. Without it, very deep models are almost impossible to train.

## Why "Norm"?

**Layer normalization** rescales each vector to have mean 0 and variance 1. It keeps the numbers in a healthy range as they pass through dozens of blocks. (Many modern models normalize *before* each sub-layer instead of after; the idea is the same.)
