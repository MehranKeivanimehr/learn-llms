## From probabilities to one token

The model's last step gives a probability for every possible next token. Something still has to **choose** one. That choice is called **sampling**, and it's controlled by a few settings you may have seen in AI tools.

- **Greedy:** always take the most likely token. Predictable, but often dull and repetitive.
- **Random sampling:** roll a weighted die. Likely tokens win most of the time, but not always — that's where variety comes from.

## The three knobs

**Temperature** reshapes the probabilities before rolling the die. Low temperature (near 0) makes the favourite even more dominant; at 0 the choice becomes greedy. High temperature flattens everything, so unlikely tokens get picked more often.

**Top-k** keeps only the *k* most likely tokens and throws the rest away.

**Top-p** (also called *nucleus sampling*) keeps the smallest group of top tokens whose probabilities add up to at least *p* — say 90% — and throws away the long tail.

## Why this matters

Low temperature is good for facts, code and anything that must be consistent. Higher temperature helps brainstorming and creative writing. Top-k and top-p let you keep some creativity while cutting off the silly long tail — like "spaceship" here.
